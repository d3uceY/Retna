# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. This project is about colors, not people scoring points off each
other.

## Scope

This applies to issues, pull requests, commit messages, code review comments,
and any other space where you are representing the project.

## Expected behavior

- Assume the other person read the code and reached a different conclusion.
  Ask which one is wrong before deciding it is them.
- Critique the change, not the author. "This clips the channel" is useful.
  "This is sloppy" is not.
- Take "no" for an answer on a design decision the maintainer has already made.
  A fork is a legitimate reply.
- Say when you do not know something. Nobody here has memorized the CIE
  standard.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks.
- Discrimination or derogatory remarks about anyone's identity, background, or
  level of experience.
- Sexual attention or imagery, in any project space.
- Deliberate intimidation, stalking, or sustained disruption of discussion.
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission.
- Dismissing a contribution because of who wrote it rather than what it does.

## Reporting

Report a problem to the maintainer, [@d3uceY](https://github.com/d3uceY), by
opening a report at <https://github.com/d3uceY/Retna/issues> and marking it as
a conduct report, or by contacting the maintainer directly on GitHub. Do not
open a public issue with the details. Reports are read by the maintainer and
nobody else.

## Enforcement

The maintainer will decide what happens, ranging from a private word to a
permanent ban from the project's spaces. Anything short of a warning will
usually come with a short explanation. Reports are handled in confidence, and
the reporter's account of events is not required to be the only account
considered.
